## Triple sharing

`T3CentralRootRelationsPublic` computes the normal closure of central graph relations `(z_i⁻¹,w_i)` as their ordinary generated subgroup. Every relation element has an actual integer-exponent product representation in a commutative subgroup of the product. Independent second-coordinate functionals recover the exponents modulo three; the exponent-three law then kills every factor when the second coordinate is trivial. This proves actual kernel triviality on the base factor, rather than only injectivity on a graded quotient.

`T3SharedTripleRoots` applies this mechanism to `(G × Free (Fin 4))/L`; its `baseMap_injective` at line 183 and `baseMap_root` at line 191 give the original group embedding and all four specified root equations. Centrality of the four supplied elements is the source assumption; no independence or nontriviality of them is imposed.

`T3SharedLowerCentralStrictification` uses the same quotient and `D = image(C × Free (Fin 4))`. The quotient kernel lies inside `C × F`, which justifies the actual subgroup intersection transport; its third ambient-term and intrinsic-derived containment are separately proved at lines 111 and 127. `isStrict_enlargedSubgroup` at line 183 takes precisely the existing degree-two intersection equality and the generating equality for the third-layer defect, then proves both strictness equalities. The endpoint at line 300 adds at most four generators, gives rank at most rank C + 4, and supplies the embedding and `IsStrict` in the same universe. Finite generation is required only for the numerical-rank package.

## Commutator sharing

The pair index map is injective, so these are independent actual second-layer classes. Claim A is reused from the existing simultaneous normal-word proof; no single-root iteration is substituted.

`coefficients_eq_zero` at line 244 projects the second-layer relation equation to the free factor and obtains each integer coefficient modulo three. The exponent-three law turns this into an actual product of central relator commutators. `relator_bracket_blocks` at line 360 computes the four degree-three blocks with signs `(-[u,s], -u⊗t, -s⊗q, [q,t])`, matching the canonical mixed-bracket conventions. In `centralWord_eq_one` at line 406, the third block of a pure base element is zero, so independence of the q_i implies every s_i is zero. The first block then vanishes, and γ₄(G)=1 implies the original base element is trivial. The endpoint `baseMap_injective` is at line 468.

It does not claim the second strictness equality that Lemma 4.7 itself does not supply.
