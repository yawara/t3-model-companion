# Palomar preparation

This repository contains the substantive Lean formalization of
*Existence of a Model Companion for Groups of Exponent 3*.
The formalization's author and responsible maintainer is Yawara Ishida.
The manuscript's authors are Yawara Ishida, Ryosuke Mizuno, and Kota Takeuchi.
The structured account is [formalization.yaml](../formalization.yaml).

Public availability and the exact submitted commit will be decided separately.

## Mathematical claims

The proposed [Comparator configuration](../comparator.json) selects two
declarations from the manuscript. Throughout, an exponent-three group means
a group satisfying `x^3 = 1` for every element, so the trivial group is included.

1. **A uniformly bounded obstruction to amalgamation**
   (`T3.exists_bounded_nonamalgamation_witness`, Theorem 3.3,
   `Paper-ID: main.bounded_witness`, TeX label `thm:main`). Let `M` be an
   existentially closed exponent-three group, `A` a finite subgroup of `M`,
   and `j : A -> B` an embedding into an exponent-three group generated by
   at most `m` elements. If `M` and `B` cannot be embedded into a common
   exponent-three group with the two embeddings agreeing on `A`, this failure
   already occurs for a subgroup `A <= D <= M` with at most `f(m)` generators,
   where

   ```text
   t(m) = m + choose(m, 2) + choose(m, 3)
   f(m) = 15 * ((3*m + 4) * t(m) + 1)^2.
   ```

   This is the explicit function obtained in the paper's proof. It does not
   depend on `A`. The Lean signature uses finite generation of `A`; this is
   equivalent to finiteness here because exponent-three groups are locally
   finite. The embedding `j` represents the paper's inclusion of `A` in `B`.
   Existential closedness means that existential formulas over `M` realized
   in extension models are already realized in `M`.

2. **Existence of a model companion** (`T3.has_model_companion`, Corollary 3.4,
   `Paper-ID: main.model_companion`). The first-order theory `T3` of groups
   satisfying `x^3 = 1` has a model companion. A companion has models into
   which the models of `T3` embed, and conversely. Model completeness is
   expressed as equivalence, over the companion theory, of every formula to
   an existential formula. The proof supplies the bounded-amalgamation
   hypothesis from the first theorem; no unproved structural hypothesis is
   added to the conclusion.

The [source manuscript](../T3_modelcompanion_v7.tex) is identified by SHA256
`fb32d367e325f081eaeaf77b0c680662c9f58d1e8bc2a45adb0436cbe17c3ad6`.
No DOI, arXiv identifier, or external publication status is asserted.

## Statement boundary and fidelity

[Challenge.lean](../Challenge.lean) is the independent, readable statement
module. It imports Mathlib rather than the proof library, spells out the
needed mathematical definitions, and leaves deliberate proof holes for the
two advertised theorems. [Solution.lean](../Solution.lean) obtains the same
declarations from the completed proof development. Comparator is responsible
for checking their agreement and permitted axiom dependencies.

Neither module is part of the mathematical library's import aggregation.
The deliberate Challenge holes are excluded from the proof-development
`sorry_count`; the mathematical library and the proved Solution must remain
free of holes. The permitted axioms are `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and
`Quot.sound`.

The [paper map](paper-map.md) tracks 60 v7 items and 80 parts,
including supporting facts, examples, remarks, universe bridges, and Section 6.
The two questions and Takeuchi's conjecture are explicitly marked open.
Their registration does not assert a proof, and the new items' implementation
states are recorded individually. The
Comparator selection covers the two principal results above. A successful
comparison of those declarations does not by itself independently certify
every natural-language correspondence in the paper map.

The implementation preserves the paper's constants and principal
constructions. Embeddings encode inclusions, and proved universe bridges
connect the canonical model-theoretic definitions with models in arbitrary
universes. An auxiliary degree-one comparison uses a direct proof by
universality and retraction. These are documented encoding and proof choices;
they do not supply extra mathematical hypotheses. Editorial placeholders in
the manuscript are outside the formalized mathematical scope.

The additional Section 6 research notes on
[finite residuals](../notes/section-six-finite-residual.md),
[coprime joins](../notes/section-six-coprime-joins.md),
[finite obstruction criteria](../notes/section-six-obstruction-criteria.md), and
[finite A-groups](../notes/section-six-a-groups.md) contain ordinary mathematical
arguments and literature analysis, not additional Lean-verified results or
Comparator declarations. They do not assert solutions to the general open questions.

## Production and verification

OpenAI Codex agents implemented and reviewed the formalization under Yawara
Ishida's direction. Agent fidelity reviews and Lean's kernel and axiom checks
are distinct kinds of evidence. No independent human peer review of the Lean
formalization is claimed. The manuscript separately describes its use of a
GPT-5.6 Sol proof outline and subsequent work by the paper's authors.

The [v7 verification checkpoint](../notes/v7-formalization.md) records the
2026-09-22 build, lint, import, paper-map, and axiom audits on Lean and mathlib
v4.33.1. It also records a successful comparison of the two selected statements
and their definitions, with both NanoDa and Lean's default kernel accepting
the Solution. The separate
[2026-09-10 upgrade checkpoint](../notes/lean-mathlib-4-33-1-upgrade.md)
preserves the earlier v4 verification; it does not certify the v7 additions.

The current preparation upgrades Lean and mathlib to v4.34.0. Verification of
the full project, the independent comparison, and Challenge rendering on these
pins is recorded in
[Palomar readiness, 2026-09-22](../notes/palomar-readiness-2026-09-22.md).

the earlier runs remain historical evidence for their own snapshots.
